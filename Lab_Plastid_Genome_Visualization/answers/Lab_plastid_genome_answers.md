# Plastid Genome Visualization Answers

## 1. Scientific name and accession

**Scientific name:** *Gerbera jamesonii*  
**Organelle:** Chloroplast  
**NCBI accession:** NC_046760.1  
**Genome length:** 151,898 bp

The plastid genome used for this activity is the complete chloroplast genome of *Gerbera jamesonii*.

## 2. Total plastid genome length

The complete chloroplast genome is **151,898 bp** in length.

## 3. LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions

The plastid genome has the typical quadripartite structure:

- **LSC:** 1–83,518 bp
- **IRb:** 83,519–108,586 bp
- **SSC:** 108,587–126,830 bp
- **IRa:** 126,831–151,898 bp

The organization is:

**LSC → IRb → SSC → IRa**

The two inverted repeat regions are approximately **25,068 bp each**.

## 4. Three genes located in the LSC region

Examples of genes located in the LSC region are:

- **rpoB** – encodes the beta subunit of the plastid RNA polymerase
- **rbcL** – encodes the large subunit of Rubisco
- **psbA** – encodes the photosystem II D1 protein

These genes are involved in transcription and photosynthetic functions.

## 5. One gene located in the SSC region

One example is **ndhF**.

The **ndhF** gene is part of the NADH dehydrogenase complex and is associated with photosynthetic electron transport.

## 6. One gene located in the IR region

An example is **rpl2**.

The OGDRAW map shows **rpl2 in both IR regions**, indicating that it is duplicated because genes located within the inverted repeat regions can occur in two copies.

## 7. One photosynthesis-related gene and its function

**psbA** is a photosynthesis-related gene.

It encodes the **D1 protein of photosystem II**, which is an important component of the photosynthetic reaction center and participates in the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis.

## 8. Direction of transcription

The genes are arranged in different orientations around the circular plastid genome. The arrows on the OGDRAW map indicate the **direction of transcription** for the annotated genes.

This shows that not all genes are transcribed in the same direction.

## 9. GC-content graph

The GC-content graph shows that GC content **varies across different regions of the chloroplast genome rather than remaining completely uniform**.

The graph provides a visual representation of local GC-content variation across the genome. The variation itself should not be interpreted as evidence of a specific biological cause without additional analysis.

## 10. Why is a genome map useful?

A plastid genome map makes the organization of the genome easier to understand than a raw DNA sequence or a long annotation list.

The map allows the locations and arrangement of the **LSC, SSC, IRa, IRb, protein-coding genes, tRNAs, rRNAs, and other annotated features** to be viewed at the same time. It also shows gene orientation and the GC-content pattern.

Therefore, the map provides a clear visual summary of the structure and organization of the *Gerbera jamesonii* chloroplast genome.

## Summary

The OGDRAW visualization shows a circular, 151,898-bp chloroplast genome of *Gerbera jamesonii* with the characteristic LSC–IRb–SSC–IRa organization. The map displays annotated genes, inverted repeats, gene orientations, and GC-content variation across the plastid genome.
